The growing production and improper disposal of polyethylene terephthalate (PET) is one of today's major environmental challenges. Enzymatic biodegradation — for example with PHL7, a highly efficient hydrolase isolated from compost — is a promising alternative to mechanical recycling. Microalgae are attractive hosts for recombinant proteins, and *Dunaliella salina* adds high salinity tolerance, open-pond cultivation and low contamination risk, yet it remains little explored for this purpose. This project aims to establish an efficient genetic-transformation protocol and a PHL7 expression platform in *D. salina*: optimizing culture, plating and recovery conditions and zeocin selection, comparing electroporation with glass-bead transformation, and confirming transformants by PCR. Recombinant PHL7 activity will then be tested by halo assays, zymography and PET degradation, supporting more sustainable enzymatic plastic recycling.
