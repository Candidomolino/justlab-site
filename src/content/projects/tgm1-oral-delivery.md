Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) affects 10–11% of the world's population and lacks therapies that act on its underlying mechanisms, including dysfunction of the intestinal epithelial barrier. Immunomodulatory proteins such as TGM, a TGF-β mimic from the helminth *Heligmosomoides polygyrus bakeri*, are promising candidates, but oral delivery faces gastric degradation and costly production and encapsulation. *Chlamydomonas reinhardtii*, recognized as GRAS, combines production and delivery in one system and may partly protect proteins in transit. This project builds and compares two isogenic strains expressing Hp-TGM1 fused to a His-tag and mCherry, differing only in targeting: cytosolic retention versus cell-wall anchoring. Localization and accessibility will be assessed by cell fractionation, Western blot, fluorescence microscopy and a protease assay. The goal is a design reference for oral-delivery platforms — not to demonstrate efficacy in IBS.
