Oral protein delivery must meet two opposing demands: surviving the stomach and releasing its cargo at the right intestinal site. This project addresses both with a single construct in *Chlamydomonas reinhardtii*, a low-cost phototrophic microalga whose cell wall is both the anchor and a candidate barrier against gastric conditions. The wall glycoprotein GP1 anchors two fluorescent proteins: mVenus, fused directly, as an internal retention reference, and mCherry, fused through a linker carrying the recognition site of enteropeptidase, a protease of the duodenal brush border. Intact cells are expected to keep both proteins through simulated gastric digestion (pH 3.0), and enteropeptidase to release mCherry selectively in the intestinal phase (pH 7.0). A second cassette with the P1 lysine replaced tests whether release requires the site. The result is a protease-triggered, site-specific release platform with a replaceable model cargo; immunogenicity, cell targeting and in vivo performance are not assessed.
